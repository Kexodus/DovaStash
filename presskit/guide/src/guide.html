Contents

    Reading this guide

    Each chapter is one task. Screenshots carry numbered marks; the list under the picture explains each number.

    Names you click or type in the editor look like Save template. Keys look like Ctrl S.

    The homes in the pictures

    Whitepeak Tower, Aldamar and Lakeview Manor, in a load order of about 300 plugins. Your homes and numbers will differ; the steps are the same.

    Dova Stash sorts your loot into the containers of a player home. A template tells it which container takes what in one particular home. The editor makes templates for you, outside the game.

    Open a home

    Every home of your load order, under the mod that adds it.

    Set up the chests

    Click it in 3D or on the plan: master, categories, or ignored.

    Check and save

    The same checks the game runs, then one file in its own mod.

    A template says

    • which container is the master, where anything without a place goes,
    • which categories each other container takes (potions, ingots, books…),
    • which containers to ignore (ones that respawn or belong to someone),
    • which racks, mannequins and shelves to fill.

    You need

    • Windows 10 or 11 with Direct3D 11,
    • Skyrim Special Edition or Anniversary Edition, set up with Mod Organizer 2 (or a plain Data folder),
    • Dova Stash installed. The editor comes with it.

    The editor only reads your game and mod files. It never changes a plugin. It writes template files only, into a mod of their own.

    The editor is one program, DovaStashEditor.exe. It is already in the Dova Stash download, in the mod's Tools folder. Add it to Mod Organizer 2 once, like xEdit.

    1. In MO2, open Tools › Executables (the gears icon), press + and choose Add from file…
    2. Browse to your Dova Stash mod folder, open Tools and pick DovaStashEditor.exe.
    3. Fill in the fields as below, then press OK.
    4. Pick Dova Stash Editor in MO2's run list (top right) and press Run.

    Without MO2

    Double-click the exe, or start it from a terminal with --mo2 "<MO2 folder>". Outside MO2 it rebuilds the same view of your Data folder by itself, so it gives the same answers. With no MO2 at all, point it at the game with --data "<Skyrim>\Data".

    The first start

    The editor finds MO2's instance and profile, reads your load order (about two seconds for 300 plugins) and lists your player homes. If it cannot find the instance, it asks once for the folder and remembers it.

    Tip

    To open one home straight away, add it to the arguments: --gui --home "Whitepeak Tower". --gui --mod 92177 starts with that Nexus id in the homes search.

    Everything happens in one window: your homes on the left, the home in the middle, its details on the right. Before you open a home, the middle shows the start panel.

    1. Title row. ≡ menu, the home you have open, 3D | Plan, Draft, Save and ⚙ Setup (MO2 folder, profile, author).
    2. Homes rail. Every home of your load order, under its mod (chapter 04).
    3. Start panel. Your setup, how many homes have a template, and three ways in: Open a home, Draft all homes, Open a template…
    4. Inspector. Selection, Template, Draft and Checks for the open home.
    5. Status row. Home and cell, errors and warnings, what is loading, and one useful key.

    With a home open, a drawer under it holds the wide tables (Containers, Displays, Doors, Where items go), and Ctrl K finds any command, home or container by typing. Click the emblem for the start panel again: In progress lists each home you opened, with its stage. Click one to carry on.

    Stages

    Drafted

    You opened it.

    Reviewed

    You accepted or rejected a suggestion, or edited it.

    Validated

    Reviewed, and no problems left.

    Saved

    Written to disk. The list shows where.

    Stages, your MO2 folder, author name and the window's layout are kept between runs, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\DovaStashEditor\state.json.

    You know a home by its mod. The rail on the left lists every home of your load order under the mod that adds it; a name or a Nexus id finds it.

    1. Search. Part of the home or mod name, a plugin, or the Nexus id (92177 is Whitepeak Tower).
    2. Filter: only homes without a template, only enabled mods, and more. A number shows how many are on.
    3. The mod with its Nexus id.
    4. The home: its chests and displays, and a chip: none, shipped, yours, drafted or saved. Click it to open it.
    5. All cells… of this mod, for a home that is not listed.
    6. Rescan (F5) and what the last scan read.

    How the editor finds homes

    It reads every cell of your load order once and keeps the homes: a player-house location, owned by the player, or mannequins and nothing that respawns. Each sits under the mod whose plugin adds it; base-game and DLC homes sit under Game Data folder. Rescan reads it again after you change mods in MO2.

    A home owned by a faction, or an exterior, is not listed. Click All cells… under its mod: every cell of its plugins with containers is ranked, likely homes first. Untick Likely homes only in the filter to see them all.

    The rail stays beside the home while you go from one to the next; ↑ ↓ and Enter work too, and right-click a home for more. Ctrl 1 or its ‹ folds it to a strip. The home's name in the title row is the same list, for a quick switch.

    The 3D view

    The home as it looks in game, built from your own meshes and textures. It opens from above while the meshes load; the status row shows the progress.

    1. The home. Hover anything to see its name; click to select it.
    2. Inspector. With nothing selected: Make the nearest chest the master, Draft this home, Open the containers list.
    3. Hint line: the controls for the mode you are in.
    4. Select picks what a template can use; S lets it pick anything. Paint: chapter 07.
    5. Layers (L). Hide structure, people, disabled upgrades or effects. Height cut shows one floor of a tower.
    6. Colours (chapter 10) and Camera: Overview (Home), Frame (F), speed, light.
    7. 3D | Plan (1 2), in the title row.
    What the colours mean

    Things you cannot see in game but a template can use, such as hidden chests and rack triggers, are drawn as see-through boxes: green containers, red respawning containers, blue weapon racks, magenta mannequins, orange plaques and cases, yellow doors, lavender furniture. A gray checker is a texture the editor could not find; it does not matter for the template.

    Press Plan in the title row (2). The plan draws every object's footprint from above, north up. It is the fastest way to find a room or pick something that is hard to reach in 3D. Plan and 3D view share one selection.

    1. Select and Paint, as in the 3D view.
    2. Layers: structure, containers, displays, doors, furniture, other, and Height cut: the lowest and highest heights shown. Narrow it to see one floor.
    3. Colours and Camera, as in the 3D view. Home fits the whole home in view.
    4. The selection, marked on the plan.
    5. Scale bar, in game units.
    6. North.

    On the plan

    • Mouse wheel zooms, right or middle drag pans.
    • Left click selects, left drag draws a box. Press S to make left drag pan instead.
    • Hover a footprint to see its name.

    Then in 3D

    Select a chest on the plan, press 1 for the 3D view and F. The camera finds a side of it where no wall is in the way.

    Click a container in 3D, on the plan or in the drawer's Containers tab. The inspector's Selection panel shows what it is, its reference and base object, then, under Role, its job in the template.

    1. Make master: this container takes everything without a place of its own, and is the hotkey’s target. A home has one master; the old one becomes a plain container.
    2. Label: the name players see. It starts as the game's name.
    3. Ignored: Dova Stash never uses it.
    4. Categories it takes. + add opens the category tree; × on a chip removes one.
    5. Note for people who read the file.
    6. Remove from the template: it is no longer listed (unlisted containers stay unused).
    Choosing the master

    Pick a large chest near the front door that never respawns. It is the home’s inbox: whatever has no container of its own ends up there. The master’s role shows a master chip and offers Not the master.

    Categories

    Categories come from Dova Stash's category files, the same ones the game uses. They form a tree: alchemy.potions, misc.ores_ingots, weapons.staves. A name ending in .* takes the whole group, so weapons.* takes every weapon. When two containers take the same items, the more exact category wins.

    Ignore what is not safe

    1. Respawns. Containers that reset lose what you put in them. The editor marks them in red and never makes one the master.
    2. Ignored is ticked.
    3. The reason goes in the note, so other players know why.

    Ignore containers that respawn, belong to someone else, or disappear with a house upgrade. The draft (chapter 09) does this for you and writes the reason.

    Every container in one table: the drawer

    1. Role: its categories, no categories, ignored or not listed.
    2. Reference. Click a row to select it; double-click to look at it in 3D. Hidden chests you cannot click are reachable here.
    Good to know

    A container with no categories still matters: it stays empty, and its items go to the master. In Where items go (chapter 10) it shows as receives nothing. Ctrl 2 opens or folds the drawer; drag its top edge to make it taller.

    Paint mode

    Press P (or Paint in the toolbar), pick a category, then click containers, racks and mannequins to give it to them. Click again to take it away. The master, respawning and ignored containers tell you why they cannot be painted.

    1. Paint on: the paint bar, with the category to paint, Pick another and Done.
    2. By category colours; the key is under Colours.
    3. Click a container to give it the category; click again to take it away.
    4. Hint line: what a click does now. P or S stops painting.
    Tip

    Paint a room at a time: narrow Layers › Height cut to one floor, pick a category, and click every chest that should take it. Painted containers change colour at once, so you see what is left. While painting, Ctrl K and a few letters pick the next category.

    Undo and redo

    Every change to the template can be undone: Ctrl Z undoes, Ctrl Y (or Ctrl Shift Z) redoes, or use Undo and Redo in the ≡ menu. A text field counts as one step once you leave it. The editor keeps 200 steps, also while you move between the home's cells.

    Multi-select

    Ctrl + click adds or removes one object, in 3D, on the plan or in the drawer's tables. Drag a box to select every container and display inside it (hold Ctrl to add). Esc clears the selection; Delete takes it out of the template.

    1. Three sacks, each in its own frame.
    2. How many are selected, by kind.
    3. Categories of the selection, with how many have each (1 of 3). x takes it from all.
    4. Give all of them a category, or Clear their categories.
    5. Ignore all or Use all.
    6. One note for all of them (Set on all).
    7. Clear the selection (Esc).

    Unsaved changes

    While a template has changes that are not saved, a dot follows the home's name in the title row, in the status row and in the rail, and the chip next to its name reads draft. Opening another home keeps them: the dot stays on its row until you save. Closing the window asks first.

    Racks, mannequins, plaques, display cases and bookshelves are displays. Dova Stash fills the ones the template uses with your best gear. Nothing is ever taken off a display.

    1. The mannequin, selected.
    2. Rack triggers, drawn as blue see-through boxes so you can click them.
    3. Display: mannequin and the script that makes it one.
    4. Use this display: Dova Stash fills it. Untick to leave it alone.
    5. Armor set: Auto wears your most complete set; pick one (type to search every set of your load order) and it wears only that set, filling up as you get its pieces.
    6. Categories, for Auto only. Empty: any set.
    1. Kind of rack: any weapon, no daggers or bows, daggers, or a shield.
    2. Use this display.
    3. Categories: armor.shields here; a bow rack could take weapons.bows.
    4. Empty means any weapon that fits the rack.

    Plaques and display cases

    Each keeps what its own script was made for, such as a dragon priest mask. The editor lists those items for you.

    Bookshelves

    Listed in the drawer's Displays tab with the racks and plaques. Give one categories (skill books, spell tomes, notes) or leave it empty for the books most worth showing.

    You never start from nothing: a home opens with a draft, a template the editor made from the home itself. The inspector's Draft panel shows what it suggests and why. Open it with the Draft tab, Draft › Draft this home in the title row, or right-click a home in the rail.

    1. Accept all, Reject the weak ones (under 50%), Draft again (from the home's plugins).
    2. The master and why it was chosen. Hover it for the whole reason and the next best.
    3. Suggested categories per container, as chips with a confidence. Click a chip to accept or reject it; hover it to read its reason. Click a container's name to find it in the view.
    4. Displays the draft uses, folded at the end. Problems are in the Checks panel.

    Colours and confidence

    Green: the container's name says so. Yellow: a crafting station or items nearby. Orange: a weaker hint, such as the kind of furniture. The number is the confidence. Each category goes to one container and is accepted from 50%; the chips left off are the alternatives (Potion Basket has it with 93%).

    The Draft panel works on the template itself: a chip you accept is a category of that container at once, in the Selection panel, the checks and Where items go. Ctrl Z takes it back.

    Draft all homes

    Ctrl D (or Draft all homes in the ≡ and Draft menus, or on the start panel) drafts every home of your load order at once. The status row shows the progress; you can keep working.

    1. Search and filter: Only homes without a template finds the ones still to do.
    2. Save N drafts… saves every draft of a home without a template at once, after one question.
    3. Every home with its chip: drafted once it has a draft. Open one to review it in the Draft panel.

    Where the suggestions come from

    The name

    A container called Soul Gems, Ingots or Potion Basket takes those, and only those. Homes with named containers come out nearly complete. A name the editor cannot read, such as Dragonpriest Masks, gets nothing: you decide.

    What is near it

    A chest by the forge gets ores and ingots; one by the alchemy lab, ingredients and potions. Items placed on it add to that; decoration, like a shield on the wall, says nothing.

    The master

    A chest near the front door, never one named for what it holds. Containers that respawn, are owned, or are scripted are never chosen.

    The rest

    Common groups nobody took (clothes, books, food…) go to the free container with the best hint, such as a dresser for clothes or a safe for jewelry.

    Reviewing a draft

    1. Open the home from the rail and pick the Draft panel.
    2. Read the master and its reason. If it is wrong, select another container and press Make master.
    3. Go down the containers. Click the chips that fit on, the others off. Reject the weak ones clears the low ones in one go.
    4. Finish in the same window: rename labels, set up displays, check Where items go.
    5. Press Save (Ctrl S). Or review several homes and save them all with Save N drafts… (homes without a template only): each keeps its draft while you open the next.
    Homes that already have a template

    The Draft panel says how many categories the draft sends to the same container as the existing template, and whether the master is the same. Saving asks first; the old file is kept as a backup.

    The drawer's Where items go tab runs Dova Stash's own sorting over a sample of loot and shows which container would get what, with the home still in view above it. It runs again by itself whenever the template changes.

    1. Sample: Typical haul, One category, or Every item in the load order.
    2. Run again (R).
    3. Colours for the 3D view and the plan: By fill, By category.
    4. Summary: how much goes to containers, the master and displays.
    5. The master: what has no other place. Here, nothing.
    6. Fill and item counts, per category.
    7. Receives nothing, and why: give it categories or ignore it.

    Samples

    Typical haulA handful of each kind of loot, base-game items first (about 110 stacks). The quick everyday check.
    One categoryEvery item of your load order in one category, such as alchemy.*: does all of it land where you expect?
    Every item in the load orderOne of each playable item, modded ones included. Shows what still falls to the master.

    See it in the home

    Press By fill (V): every container in the 3D view or on the plan takes the colour of how much it would get. Click one to list its items in the Selection panel, under Where its items go.

    1. The key, under Colours: gray receives nothing, green a few (under 20), orange many, magenta the master.
    2. Containers that get a few items.
    3. Master container, selected.
    4. Its preview: nothing falls to the master, so every item found a place.
    1. The same key on the plan.
    2. The master, in magenta.

    By category

    Press By category (C) to colour each container by its first category instead, one hue per group: you see at a glance where the alchemy, the weapons and the materials live.

    Many homes are more than one cell: a cellar, a tower, the grounds with the forge. The drawer's Doors tab lists where the home's doors lead, so you can add those cells to the same home. A home the editor already knows as several cells opens with all of them.

    1. Open the cell behind the door. The template comes along; Back (‹ in the title row) returns.
    2. In the home: tick to make that cell part of this home.
    3. Where it leads: another interior, or the exterior cells around the home.

    Adding a cell

    1. Open the home, then Doors in the drawer.
    2. Tick In the home next to the cellar, or press Open, select a chest there and press Add this cell to the home.
    3. Set up its containers as usual. In 3D, double-click a door to walk through it.

    One file per home

    However many cells, the home is saved as one template. Its cells are listed in the Template panel with their names (Cellar, Grounds), which players see in game; × takes one out.

    Exteriors open with their terrain and the cells around them, so you can find the forge outside.

    1. The shipped template and how far the draft agrees with it.
    2. Another cell: a container there says so. Click it to open that cell.

    The template is checked the way the game will check it while you work. The status row counts errors and warnings; click it for the Checks panel. The Template panel holds the template's details and saves it.

    1. Name, Author, Version, Priority, Note. Your author name is remembered.
    2. Summary: master, containers with categories, ignored, displays, plugins required.
    3. Cells of the home.
    4. Keep rules for this home: what players always or never keep on them, and how many of a category. None: their own rules apply.
    5. Save to: the mod it saves into, and the file.
    6. Save (Ctrl S), Save as… a copy anywhere, Copy JSON.

    Checks lists the problems, errors first: click one to select the object. Fix every error before you share; Save waits until there are none. Under Still to do it lists what the template lacks, such as containers without a role.

    Where it goes

    Into a mod of its own, Dova Stash Templates, created when missing:

    mods\Dova Stash Templates\SKSE\Plugins\DovaStash\Templates\<Author>\<Home>.json

    Never MO2's overwrite folder, so cleaning overwrite cannot delete it.

    Then

    1. In MO2, press F5 and enable Dova Stash Templates (first save only).
    2. In game, look at a container in that home, press the hotkey and pick the template.

    Saving over a file asks first, keeps the old one as a .bak and raises the version (1.0.0, 1.0.1…). ⚙ Setup picks another mod to save into.

    It says it cannot find my MO2 instance.

    Type the MO2 folder on the start panel and press Open, or use Browse…. Or add --mo2 "<MO2 folder>" to the executable's arguments.

    The wrong profile is loaded.

    Pick another under ⚙ Setup › Profile. The editor reads that profile's mod list and load order.

    My home is not in the list.

    Check the mod is enabled in the profile, then Rescan. Still missing: click All cells… under its mod, and in the filter untick Likely homes only or tick Exteriors.

    I cannot click a chest.

    It may be hidden or behind a wall. Select it in the drawer's Containers tab, or turn off Structure under Layers.

    Where is that button?

    Press Ctrl K and type what you want: a command, a home, a container.

    Some surfaces are a gray checker.

    The editor could not find that texture. It does not affect the template.

    Make master is grayed out.

    That container respawns. Its contents can vanish, so it can never be the master; ignore it.

    A container “receives nothing”.

    It has no categories, or none of the sample matches them. Give it categories, or ignore it.

    My template does not show up in game.

    Press F5 in MO2 and enable Dova Stash Templates. Then fix any errors in its Checks panel.

    I saved over a template by mistake.

    The old file is next to it as a .bak. Rename it back to .json.

    Can it break my game?

    No. It never edits plugins or game files. It only writes template files into its own mod.

    Can I edit a template I already have?

    Yes: Open a template… (Ctrl O) opens a shipped or downloaded template, or a setup you saved in game, on its home.

    How do I share a template?

    Upload the .json file, keeping its SKSE\Plugins\DovaStash\Templates\ folders. The template lists the plugins it needs.

    The window stays black.

    The editor needs Direct3D 11. Update your graphics driver.

    Where are my settings kept?

    In %LOCALAPPDATA%\DovaStashEditor\state.json: the MO2 folder, profile, author, each home's stage and the window's layout.
