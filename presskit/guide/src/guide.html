Contents

    Reading this guide

    Each chapter is one task. Screenshots carry numbered marks; the list under the picture explains each number.

    Names you click or type in the editor look like Save template. Keys look like Ctrl S.

    The homes in the pictures

    Whitepeak Tower, Aldamar and Lakeview Manor, in a load order of about 300 plugins. Your homes and numbers will differ; the steps are the same.

    Dova Stash sorts your loot into the containers of a player home. A template tells it which container takes what in one particular home. The editor makes templates for you, outside the game.

    Open a home

    Every home of your load order, under the mod that adds it.

    Set up the chests

    Click it in 3D or on the plan: master, categories, or ignored.

    Check and save

    The same checks the game runs, then one file in its own mod.

    A template says

    • which container is the master, where anything without a place goes,
    • which categories each other container takes (potions, ingots, books…),
    • which containers to ignore (ones that respawn or belong to someone),
    • which racks, mannequins and shelves to fill.

    You need

    • Windows 10 or 11 with Direct3D 11,
    • Skyrim Special Edition or Anniversary Edition, set up with Mod Organizer 2 (or a plain Data folder),
    • Dova Stash installed. The editor comes with it.

    The editor only reads your game and mod files. It never changes a plugin. It writes template files only, into a mod of their own.

    The editor is one program, DovaStashEditor.exe. It is already in the Dova Stash download, in the mod's Tools folder. Add it to Mod Organizer 2 once, like xEdit.

    1. In MO2, open Tools › Executables (the gears icon), press + and choose Add from file…
    2. Browse to your Dova Stash mod folder, open Tools and pick DovaStashEditor.exe.
    3. Fill in the fields as below, then press OK.
    4. Pick Dova Stash Editor in MO2's run list (top right) and press Run.

    Without MO2

    Double-click the exe, or start it from a terminal with --mo2 "<MO2 folder>". Outside MO2 it rebuilds the same view of your Data folder by itself, so it gives the same answers. With no MO2 at all, point it at the game with --data "<Skyrim>\Data".

    The first start

    The editor finds MO2's instance and profile, reads your load order (about a second for 300 plugins) and counts your player homes. If it cannot find the instance, it asks once for the folder and remembers it.

    Tip

    To open one home straight away, add it to the arguments: --gui --mod 92177 --cell bcr58WhiteTower opens Whitepeak Tower (Nexus id 92177).

    Every session starts here. It shows that your setup was found, how many homes your load order has, and the homes you are working on.

    1. Your setup. The MO2 instance, profile and Data folder in use. Change them in the bar at the top (Browse…, Profile).
    2. Player homes. Every home in your active load order, and how many already have a template.
    3. Three ways in. Open a home under its mod, Draft all homes at once, or Open a template… you already have.
    4. Your homes in progress. Each home you touched, with its stage. Click one to carry on.

    Stages

    Drafted

    You opened its draft.

    Reviewed

    You accepted or rejected a suggestion, or edited it.

    Validated

    Reviewed, and no problems left.

    Saved

    Written to disk. The list shows where.

    Stages, your MO2 folder and your author name are kept between runs, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\DovaStashEditor\state.json.

    You know a home by its mod. Open a home lists every home of your load order under the mod that adds it; a name or a Nexus id finds it.

    1. Homes by mod tab.
    2. By mod lists the homes; Any cell is for a home that is not listed.
    3. Filter. Part of the home or mod name, or the Nexus id (92177 is Whitepeak Tower).
    4. The mod with its Nexus id. Point at it to see its plugins.
    5. The home with its storage and template. Click it to open it.

    How the editor finds homes

    It reads every cell of your load order once and keeps the homes: a player-house location, owned by the player, or mannequins and nothing that respawns. Each sits under the mod whose plugin adds it; base-game and DLC homes sit under (Game Data folder). Rescan (F5) reads it again after you change mods in MO2.

    A home owned by a faction, or an exterior, is not listed. In Any cell, pick its mod: every cell of its plugins with containers is ranked, likely homes first. Untick Likely homes only to see them all.

    The list stays beside the cell while you go from one home to the next. Hide lists, at the top left of the cell, gives the cell the whole window; Homes brings it back. A home opened from Draft all homes or a template file starts with the list folded away.

    The 3D view

    The home as it looks in game, built from your own meshes and textures. It opens from above while the meshes load.

    1. The home. Hover anything to see its name; click to select it.
    2. Details pane. With nothing selected it shows the colour key.
    3. Hint bar: the controls for the mode you are in.
    4. Tabs for this cell: 3D view, Floor plan, Containers, Displays, Doors, Template, Where items go.
    5. Select mode (S): on, a click picks what a template can use. Overview (Home), Frame (F), Speed, Light.
    6. Layers. Hide structure, people, disabled upgrades or effects. Height cuts a tower into floors.
    7. Paint and Colours: see chapters 07 and 10.
    What the colours mean

    Things you cannot see in game but a template can use, such as hidden chests and rack triggers, are drawn as see-through boxes: green containers, red respawning containers, blue weapon racks, magenta mannequins, orange plaques and cases, yellow doors, lavender furniture. A gray checker is a texture the editor could not find; it does not matter for the template.

    The plan draws every object's footprint from above, north up. It is the fastest way to find a room or pick something that is hard to reach in 3D. Plan and 3D view share one selection.

    1. Select mode, Fit (the whole cell in view) and Height: the lowest and highest heights shown. Narrow it to see one floor.
    2. Layers: structure, containers, displays, doors, furniture, other.
    3. Colours, as in the 3D view.
    4. The selection, marked on the plan.
    5. Scale bar, in game units.
    6. North.

    On the plan

    • Mouse wheel zooms, right or middle drag pans.
    • Left click selects. With select mode off, left drag pans.
    • Hover a footprint to see its name.

    Then in 3D

    Select a chest on the plan, switch to 3D view and press F. The camera finds a side of it where no wall is in the way.

    Click a container in 3D, on the plan or in the Containers tab. Its details open on the right: what it is, its reference and base object, then, under In the template, the role editor.

    1. Make master: this container takes everything without a place of its own, and is the hotkey’s target. A home has one master; the old one becomes a plain container.
    2. Label: the name players see. It starts as the game's name.
    3. Ignored: Dova Stash never uses it.
    4. Categories it takes. Add a category… opens the category tree; x removes one.
    5. Note for people who read the file.
    6. Remove from the template: it is no longer listed (unlisted containers stay unused).
    Choosing the master

    Pick a large chest near the front door that never respawns. It is the home’s inbox: whatever has no container of its own ends up there. The master’s details say Master container and offer Not the master.

    Categories

    Categories come from Dova Stash's category files, the same ones the game uses. They form a tree: alchemy.potions, misc.ores_ingots, weapons.staves. A name ending in .* takes the whole group, so weapons.* takes every weapon. When two containers take the same items, the more exact category wins.

    Ignore what is not safe

    1. Respawns: never bind it. Containers that reset lose what you put in them. The editor marks them in red.
    2. Ignored is ticked.
    3. The reason goes in the note, so other players know why.

    Ignore containers that respawn, belong to someone else, or disappear with a house upgrade. The draft (chapter 09) does this for you and writes the reason.

    Every container in one table

    1. Role: its categories, no categories, ignored or not listed.
    2. Reference. Click a row to select it; double-click to look at it in 3D. Hidden chests you cannot click are reachable here.
    Good to know

    A container with no categories still matters: it stays empty, and its items go to the master. In Where items go (chapter 10) it shows as receives nothing.

    Paint mode

    Press P (or tick Paint), pick a category, then click containers, racks and mannequins to give it to them. Click again to take it away. The master, respawning and ignored containers tell you why they cannot be painted.

    1. Paint on, with the category to paint.
    2. By category colours and their key.
    3. Click a container to give it the category; click again to take it away.
    4. Hint bar: what a click does now. P stops painting.
    Tip

    Paint a room at a time: narrow Height to one floor, pick a category, and click every chest that should take it. Painted containers change colour at once, so you see what is left.

    Undo and redo

    Every change to the template can be undone: Ctrl Z undoes, Ctrl Y (or Ctrl Shift Z) redoes, or use Undo and Redo next to the home's name. A text field counts as one step once you leave it. The editor keeps 200 steps, also while you move between the home's cells.

    Multi-select

    Ctrl + click adds or removes one object, in 3D, on the plan or in the tables. In select mode, drag a box to select every container and display inside it (hold Ctrl to add). Esc clears the selection.

    1. Three sacks, each in its own frame.
    2. How many are selected, by kind.
    3. Categories of the selection, with how many have each (1 of 3). x takes it from all.
    4. Give all of them a category, or Clear their categories.
    5. Ignore all or Use all.
    6. One note for all of them.
    7. Clear the selection (Esc).

    Unsaved changes

    While a template has changes that are not saved, the top bar says * unsaved changes, the cell header says (template not saved) and the Template tab has a star. Closing the window asks first.

    Racks, mannequins, plaques, display cases and bookshelves are displays. Dova Stash fills the ones the template uses with your best gear. Nothing is ever taken off a display.

    1. The mannequin, selected.
    2. Rack triggers, drawn as blue see-through boxes so you can click them.
    3. Display: mannequin and the script that makes it one.
    4. Use this display: Dova Stash fills it. Untick to leave it alone.
    5. Armor set: Auto wears your most complete set; pick one (type to search every set of your load order) and it wears only that set, filling up as you get its pieces.
    6. Categories, for Auto only. Empty: any set.
    1. Kind of rack: any weapon, no daggers or bows, daggers, or a shield.
    2. Use this display.
    3. Categories: armor.shields here; a bow rack could take weapons.bows.
    4. Empty means any weapon that fits the rack.

    Plaques and display cases

    Each keeps what its own script was made for, such as a dragon priest mask. The editor lists those items for you.

    Bookshelves

    Listed in the Displays tab with the racks and plaques. Give one categories (skill books, spell tomes, notes) or leave it empty for the books most worth showing.

    Instead of starting from nothing, let the editor draft a template for every home in your load order. Open it from the start page, the top bar or Ctrl D.

    1. The home, whether it has a template, and the actions: Open in the editor (Enter), Save template (Ctrl S), Accept all, Reject the weak ones, Draft again.
    2. The master and why it was chosen, with the next best.
    3. Suggested categories per container, as checkboxes with a confidence. Hover one to read its reason.
    4. Displays used (click to open the list) and Checks: the draft against the game's own rules.

    Colours and confidence

    Green: the container's name says so. Yellow: a crafting station or items nearby. Orange: a weaker hint, such as the kind of furniture. The number is the confidence; hover a suggestion to read its reason. Each category goes to one container and is ticked from 50%. Unticked ones are the alternatives (Potion Basket has it with 93%).

    The list of homes

    1. Filter by home, plugin or cell; Only homes without a template; Rescan (F5).
    2. Your author name, and Save N drafts… for every opened draft at once.
    3. Every home, its template (none, shipped, yours) and its stage. ↑ ↓ step through them.

    Where the suggestions come from

    The name

    A container called Soul Gems, Ingots or Potion Basket takes those, and only those. Homes with named containers come out nearly complete. A name the editor cannot read, such as Dragonpriest Masks, gets nothing: you decide.

    What is near it

    A chest by the forge gets ores and ingots; one by the alchemy lab, ingredients and potions. Items placed on it add to that; decoration, like a shield on the wall, says nothing.

    The master

    A chest near the front door, never one named for what it holds. Containers that respawn, are owned, or are scripted are never chosen.

    The rest

    Common groups nobody took (clothes, books, food…) go to the free container with the best hint, such as a dresser for clothes or a safe for jewelry.

    Reviewing a draft

    1. Read the master and its reason. If it is wrong, open the home and make another container the master.
    2. Go down the containers. Tick what fits, untick what does not. Reject the weak ones clears the low ones in one go.
    3. Press Open in the editor to finish in 3D: rename labels, set up displays, check Where items go. Your changes come back to the drafts view.
    4. Press Save template. Or open several drafts and save them all with Save N drafts… (homes without a template only).
    Homes that already have a template

    The drafts view says how many categories the draft sends to the same container as the existing template. Saving asks first; the old file is kept as a backup.

    The Where items go tab runs Dova Stash's own sorting over a sample of loot and shows which container would get what. It runs again by itself whenever the template changes.

    1. Sample: Typical haul, One category, or Every item in the load order.
    2. Run again (R).
    3. Colours for the 3D view and the plan.
    4. Summary: how much goes to containers, the master and displays.
    5. The master: what has no other place. Here, nothing.
    6. Fill and item counts, per category.
    7. Receives nothing, and why: give it categories or ignore it.

    Samples

    Typical haulA handful of each kind of loot, base-game items first (about 110 stacks). The quick everyday check.
    One categoryEvery item of your load order in one category, such as alchemy.*: does all of it land where you expect?
    Every item in the load orderOne of each playable item, modded ones included. Shows what still falls to the master.

    See it in the home

    Tick by fill (V) and go back to the 3D view or the plan: every container takes the colour of how much it would get. Click one to list its items in the details pane.

    1. The key: gray receives nothing, green a few (under 20), orange many, magenta the master.
    2. Containers that get a few items.
    3. Master container, selected.
    4. Its preview: nothing falls to the master, so every item found a place.
    1. The same key on the plan.
    2. The master, in magenta.

    By category

    Tick by category (C) to colour each container by its first category instead, one hue per group: you see at a glance where the alchemy, the weapons and the materials live.

    Many homes are more than one cell: a cellar, a tower, the grounds with the forge. The Doors tab lists where the home's doors lead, so you can add those cells to the same home.

    1. Open the cell behind the door. The template comes along; Back returns.
    2. In the home: tick to make that cell part of this home.
    3. Where it leads: another interior, or the exterior cells around the home.

    Adding a cell

    1. Open the home's main cell, then the Doors tab.
    2. Tick In the home next to the cellar, or press Open and then Add this cell to the home in the details pane.
    3. Set up its containers as usual. In 3D, double-click a door to walk through it.

    One file per home

    However many cells, the home is saved as one template. Its cells are listed in the Template tab with their names (Cellar, Grounds), which players see in game.

    Exteriors open with their terrain and the cells around them, so you can find the forge outside.

    1. More cells of this home, found from their doors.
    2. The shipped template and how far the draft agrees with it.
    3. Cell: which cell each container is in.

    The Template tab holds the template's details, checks it the way the game will, and saves it.

    1. Name, Author, Version, Priority, Note. Your author name is remembered.
    2. Summary: master, containers with categories, ignored, displays used, plugins required.
    3. Cells of the home.
    4. Problems: the same checks Dova Stash runs in game. Fix every error before you share.
    5. Where it saves.
    6. Save template (Ctrl S) and Copy JSON.
    7. The file as it will be written.

    Where it goes

    Into a mod of its own, Dova Stash Templates, created when missing:

    mods\Dova Stash Templates\SKSE\Plugins\DovaStash\Templates\<Author>\<Home>.json

    Never MO2's overwrite folder, so cleaning overwrite cannot delete it.

    Then

    1. In MO2, press F5 and enable Dova Stash Templates (first save only).
    2. In game, look at a container in that home, press the hotkey and pick the template.

    Saving over a file asks first, keeps the old one as a .bak and raises the version (1.0.0, 1.0.1…).

    It says it cannot find my MO2 instance.

    Type the MO2 folder in the top bar and press Open, or use Browse…. Or add --mo2 "<MO2 folder>" to the executable's arguments.

    The wrong profile is loaded.

    Pick another in the Profile list at the top. The editor reads that profile's mod list and load order.

    My home is not in the list.

    Check the mod is enabled in the profile, then Rescan. Still missing: in Any cell, pick its mod and untick Likely homes only, or tick Exterior cells too.

    I cannot click a chest.

    It may be hidden or behind a wall. Select it in the Containers tab, or turn off the Structure layer. Make sure Select mode is on.

    Some surfaces are a gray checker.

    The editor could not find that texture. It does not affect the template.

    Make master is grayed out.

    That container respawns. Its contents can vanish, so it can never be the master; ignore it.

    A container “receives nothing”.

    It has no categories, or none of the sample matches them. Give it categories, or ignore it.

    My template does not show up in game.

    Press F5 in MO2 and enable Dova Stash Templates. Then check its Problems list and fix any errors.

    I saved over a template by mistake.

    The old file is next to it as a .bak. Rename it back to .json.

    Can it break my game?

    No. It never edits plugins or game files. It only writes template files into its own mod.

    Can I edit a template I already have?

    Yes: Open a template… (Ctrl O) on the start page opens a shipped or downloaded template, or a setup you saved in game, on its home.

    How do I share a template?

    Upload the .json file, keeping its SKSE\Plugins\DovaStash\Templates\ folders. The template lists the plugins it needs.

    The window stays black.

    The editor needs Direct3D 11. Update your graphics driver.

    Where are my settings kept?

    In %LOCALAPPDATA%\DovaStashEditor\state.json: the MO2 folder, profile, author and each home's stage.
